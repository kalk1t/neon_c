# R3 — art direction and assembly

## Identity

Make the dam and falling water visible from the reservoir approach. Keep cyan track edges and checkpoint silhouettes readable against green cliffs and pale mist. The natural arch must have a real open flight passage. The glass observation pod anchors the human scale near the waterfall; use vegetation to frame the route without filling its clearance corridor.

The selected image sets the visual character. The route/layout is a separate playable proposal, not geometry measured from the image. Keep the recognizable landmarks even when gameplay requires changing the exact composition.

## Assembly priorities

Ravine and mesa blockout → dam and reservoir → open rock arch → vegetation → waterfall flow and foam → rain and localized mist.

Assemble F01/F03 as large cliff masses around the loop; use mesh detail only where the camera comes close. F02 requires a hollow collision opening. Repeat F04 structural dam modules and add F05 gates at planned spillways. Place F06 outside the flight corridor. Instance F07/F08 trees in small groups and F09/F12 around ledges. F10 boulders break up regular terrain. F11 is a waterfall look reference; construct water strips, foam and mist independently.

## Shared racing vocabulary

Neutral obsidian/graphite deck panels, readable brushed chrome structure, cyan navigation edges and small magenta highlights. Keep weather and distant atmosphere separate from object albedo. Use the same checkpoint language on both maps so the player's route reading stays consistent.

Use the source image to judge silhouette and the full composition, then the item PNGs to construct each piece. Do not use whole scene imagery as a model texture or replace modeled portals with billboards in a place the player can fly through.

## Scope and scale

The starter proposes a wide 18 m flight/deck corridor, 22 m gate openings and scenery scaled around 5.6 m cars. These are authoring values. Catalog dimensions describe reusable source assets; per-instance Layout dimensions can differ to fit the chosen course. Rebuild major proportions cleanly and recheck clearance when changing them.

The final scene should look coherent at driving speed. Spend detail on the gate approach, the main landmark and nearby turn edges; distant terrain/buildings need stable silhouettes more than dense bolts or leaves.
